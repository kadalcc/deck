## Run the talk from one window

<Cols cols="1fr 1fr" gap="3rem" align="start">
<Col>

<Clicks>

- A **stopwatch or a countdown** — `timer: countdown`, `duration: 30min` — that pauses, resets, fills a progress bar and goes red in overtime, with the pacing badge beside it
- **Notes you can edit** in the presenter, on `/overview` and on `/notes-edit` under `kadal-deck dev`: saved into deck.mdx, no reload
- **Markers you can click**: each `[click]` in the notes jumps to its step
- **`/overview`**: every slide with a step slider and its notes

</Clicks>

</Col>
<Col>

<How>

```mdx
---
timer: countdown
duration: 30min
---

<Notes>
Open on the problem. [click] The chart.
[click:4] Skip to the ask.
</Notes>
```

</How>

</Col>
</Cols>

<Notes>
The presenter's top bar holds the timer: click the time to pause it, the arrow to reset, the chevron to switch between stopwatch and countdown or change the length for this talk.
[click] Under `kadal-deck dev` every notes panel has an Edit button. Cmd or Ctrl+S, or clicking away, writes just this slide's notes back into the file, and the views update in place.
[click] These numbered circles are buttons: press one and the deck jumps to that step.
[click] And /overview lists every slide with a slider through its steps, the notes beside each one.
</Notes>

## Shortcuts, a right-click menu, and display settings

<Cols cols="1fr 1fr" gap="3rem" align="start">
<Col>

- **Right-click** a slide (or <Kbd>⇧</Kbd> <Kbd>F10</Kbd>): move, overview, presenter, notes, full screen, dark, pen, laser, export — and this deck's own *Back to the start*
- <Kbd>,</Kbd> opens the **display settings**: brightness, contrast, saturation, sepia, hue, invert, for a projector that needs it
- <Kbd>E</Kbd> is this deck's own shortcut — <Kbd>?</Kbd> lists it with the rest

</Col>
<Col>

<How>

```tsx
<Deck
  shortcuts={[{ keys: ["KeyE"],
    command: "view.toast",
    params: { message: "Hello" },
    description: "Say hello" }]}
  contextMenu={(items) => [...items,
    { id: "start", label: "Back to the start",
      command: "nav.first" }]}
/>
```

</How>

</Col>
</Cols>

<Notes>
Three things Slidev has that now live here too. The context menu runs the same commands as the keyboard, so it offers only what works in this window. Display settings are kept per deck in this browser and reach every window of it — tune the projector from the presenter view. And shortcuts: an array adds to the defaults, a function gets them and returns the whole list.
</Notes>

## When a slide arrives, and leaves

<Cols cols="1.2fr 1fr" gap="3rem" align="center">
<Col>

<EnterCounter />

<p className="sc-small">Go down and back up: one enter per arrival, one leave per departure. The presenter's next-slide box, the overview and print never count.</p>

</Col>
<Col>

<How>

```tsx
useSlideEnter((e) => {
  start(e.direction);
  return () => stop();
});
useSlideLeave(() => save());
```

</How>

</Col>
</Cols>

<Notes>
Slides stay mounted while they are near, so mount and unmount are not "shown". `useSlideEnter` and `useSlideLeave` are: an enter can return a cleanup that runs on leave. Slidev's names, `onSlideEnter` and `onSlideLeave`, work too.
</Notes>

## Drawings that stay

<Cols cols="1fr 1fr" gap="3rem" align="center">
<Col>

- With `drawings: { persist: "files" }`, what you pin on `kadal-deck dev` is written to `drawings/<slide>.svg`
- Saved drawings sit under your live strokes, ship with the build, and print — PDF, PNG and PPTX
- `perClick: true` ties each stroke to the click it was drawn on

</Col>
<Col>

<div className="sc-flow">
  <span>deck.mdx</span>
  <span>drawings/</span>
  <span>PDF · PPTX</span>
</div>

</Col>
</Cols>

<Notes>
The marks on this slide are not part of the slide: they are `drawings/draw-files.svg`, an ordinary SVG file the dev server wrote when they were pinned. Export the deck and they are on its page.
</Notes>

## Your face on the slide

<Clicks>

- <Kbd>V</Kbd> puts your camera in a bubble on the slide — drag it, resize it from its corner; a circle, a rounded box or a plain one, mirrored or not
- The presenter view's **Camera** shows it on every window of the deck; the arrow beside it picks the camera and the microphone
- **Record**: the screen with your voice, the camera alone, or both as two files — with pause, a clock, and WebM or MP4

</Clicks>

<Notes>
The bubble sits on the slide itself, so a recording or a screen share of the audience's view has you in it. It never prints.
[click] from the presenter view [click] and the recorder.
</Notes>

## A slide with options of its own

<Cols n={2} gap="2rem" align="start">
<Col>

This slide is `zoom: 0.8` — everything on it is set smaller, for a slide with more to show.

It carries `state: calm`, which puts a `state-calm` class on it for your CSS. And the slide after it in the source is `hide: true`: built, but never shown.

</Col>
<Col>

<How>

```yaml
---
zoom: 0.8
state: calm
hideInToc: true
---
```

```yaml
---
hide: true      # built, never shown
---
```

</How>

</Col>
</Cols>

<Notes>
Per-slide options: `zoom` scales a slide's content, `state` adds a class, `hideInToc` keeps it out of the table of contents, and `hide` leaves a slide in the source but out of the deck.
</Notes>

## Export

<Cols cols="1fr 1fr" gap="3rem" align="start">
<Col>

<How label="in the deck's folder">

```sh
kadal-deck export                  # PDF
kadal-deck export --png --pptx     # + a PNG per page, PowerPoint
kadal-deck export --scale 1 --out handouts
```

</How>

</Col>
<Col>

A headless browser prints `?print-pdf`: each PDF page is a picture of the slide with its text over it, so glass, glows and gradients look the same in every viewer and the words can still be searched. The PowerPoint is a picture per slide with your notes attached — it opens in Keynote and Google Slides looking exactly like the web.

<Callout kind="tip">Embedded pages are photographed first, so paper gets a picture where the page was.</Callout>

</Col>
</Cols>

<Notes>
Export drives a headless Chromium over the print view. PDF comes from the browser's own printer; PNGs are a screenshot of each page; the PPTX wraps those pictures one per slide with the speaker notes attached.
</Notes>
