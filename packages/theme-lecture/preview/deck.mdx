# Inner product spaces

Lengths, angles and orthogonality, from one bilinear form.

---

## Definitions, theorems and proofs

<Definition n="2.1" title="Inner product">

A map $\langle \cdot,\cdot \rangle : V \times V \to \mathbb{R}$ that is symmetric, bilinear and positive definite.

</Definition>

<Theorem n="2.2" title="Cauchy–Schwarz">

For all $u, v \in V$: $|\langle u, v\rangle| \le \lVert u\rVert\,\lVert v\rVert$.

</Theorem>

<Proof>

Expand $\lVert u - t v\rVert^2 \ge 0$ and take the discriminant in $t$ <Cite n={2} page="34" />.

</Proof>

---
layout: theorem
n: "2.3"
name: "Pythagoras"
---

If $u \perp v$, then $\lVert u + v\rVert^2 = \lVert u\rVert^2 + \lVert v\rVert^2$.

---

## Examples and remarks

<Example n="2.4">

On $\mathbb{R}^n$, the dot product $\langle x, y\rangle = \sum_i x_i y_i$ is an inner product.

</Example>

<Remark>

Every inner product induces a norm, but not every norm comes from an inner product <Cite>Halmos 1958</Cite>.

</Remark>

<Lemma n="2.5">

An orthogonal set of non-zero vectors is linearly independent.

</Lemma>
